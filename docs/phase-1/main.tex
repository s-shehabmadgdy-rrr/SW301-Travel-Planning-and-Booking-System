\section{1. Problem Statement}
Planning a trip can take a lot of time. A traveler may need one website to find a place to visit, another to look for a hotel, and a separate note or app to organize daily activities. It can be hard to keep everything in one place, especially when plans change.

Our project is a \textbf{Travel Planning and Booking System}. It will help travelers find destinations, view hotels, plan their trips, and make simple hotel reservations in one system. A traveler can also see their saved trips and bookings without searching through different websites.

The goal is to make trip planning \textbf{easier and more organized}. We will start with simple features that our team can realistically build. We can add more advanced features later.

\subsection{1.1 Who will use the system?}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Traveler:} Creates an account, explores destinations, plans a trip, and books a hotel.
\item \textbf{Admin:} Adds and updates destination and hotel information in the system.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{1.2 What is included in the first version?}
\begin{itemize}
\item User accounts and login.
\item Browse and search destinations.
\item View hotels for a destination.
\item Create a trip and add a simple list of planned activities.
\item Make, view, and cancel hotel bookings.
\item Basic admin management of destinations and hotels.
\end{itemize}

\section{3. Non-Functional Requirements}
Non-functional requirements describe how well the system should work. We chose practical targets for a small course project.

\small
\begin{longtable}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{1.12cm}|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{12.63cm}|}
\hline
\rowcolor{lightblue}\textbf{ID} & \textbf{Requirement}\\\hline
\endfirsthead
\hline\rowcolor{lightblue}\textbf{ID} & \textbf{Requirement}\\\hline
\endhead
NFR-01 & \textbf{Ease of use:} A new user should be able to find a destination and create a trip without needing instructions.\\\hline
NFR-02 & \textbf{Performance:} Common pages, such as destinations and saved trips, should load within 3 seconds in our test setup.\\\hline
NFR-03 & \textbf{Security:} Passwords must not be stored as plain text, and travelers must not access another traveler's trips or bookings.\\\hline
NFR-04 & \textbf{Reliability:} Saved trips and confirmed bookings should still be available after the user logs out and logs in again.\\\hline
NFR-05 & \textbf{Validation:} The system must show clear messages when required information is missing or booking dates are invalid.\\\hline
NFR-06 & \textbf{Maintainability:} Code should be divided into clear parts, such as users, trips, destinations, hotels, and bookings, so we can add features later.\\\hline
\end{longtable}
\normalsize

\section{4. Simple Example}
A traveler creates an account and logs in. They search for \textbf{Alexandria}, open its page, and look at available hotels. They create a trip for three days and add two activities: ``Visit the library'' and ``Walk by the sea.'' Next, they choose an available hotel room and confirm a booking. The booking appears in their account along with the saved trip.
